Reference: Announcement Categories & Financial Glossary A companion to the Build Challenge brief. You do not need a finance background — this document explains the corporate-announcement categories and the financial terms and documents referenced in the main brief. Lean on it freely.  
1. Corporate announcement categories How we classify the announcements companies file with the exchanges. In the main brief this is offered as guidance only — you may use it, adapt it, or ignore it. Category What goes in it results Quarterly/annual results, the board meeting approving them, results decks and shareholder letters, and ads confirming results were published. M&A Any acquisition, merger, demerger, or joint venture — including investing in a subsidiary, or selling a stake in one. fund_raise Raising money from outside investors — rights issues, QIPs, preferential allotments, buybacks, or debt like commercial paper. management_change Any change at the top — a CEO, CFO, MD, or director joining, resigning, or retiring. litigation Lawsuits, regulatory orders, fines, warnings, or insolvency/restructuring proceedings. insider_trading A promoter or insider buying/selling their own personal shareholding. earnings_call Anything about the investor conference call — the notice, the transcript, or the recording link. investor_meetings One-on-one or small-group meetings with specific investors/funds, or attending an investor conference. credit_rating A rating agency assigning, upgrading, downgrading, or reaffirming a credit rating. dividend A cash dividend being declared, with its record date. bonus_split A bonus issue or stock split — share count changes but no cash comes to you. capex Approving spend on a new plant, facility, or capacity expansion. press_release General news — order wins, new products, partnerships, rumour clarifications — not covered by another category. postal_ballot Shareholders voting by postal ballot — the notice and the result. agm_egm Annual or Extraordinary General Meeting — the notice and the result. pledging A promoter pledging, releasing, or changing the pledge status of shares. unclassified Routine, low-relevance paperwork — ESOP allotments, compliance certificates, ESG reports, auditor appointments — that doesn't materially move the stock.

2. Documents you'll work with Annual Report (AR) — A company's yearly disclosure. Contains the business overview, management discussion, audited financial statements (P&L, balance sheet, cash-flow), and the notes and auditor's report. The single richest primary source. Quarterly results — The financial results a company publishes every quarter (revenue, profit, etc.), filed with the exchanges. Less detailed than the annual report but far more current. Concall / earnings-call transcript — A quarterly call where management discusses the results and answers analysts' questions. The transcript is where you find guidance, tone, and what analysts are pressing on. Investor presentation — A slide deck management releases (often with results) framing strategy, KPIs, and segment performance in their own words. Exchange filing / announcement — Any disclosure a listed company files with NSE/BSE — the raw feed the push side monitors. screener.in — A widely-used site that consolidates a company's annual reports, concall transcripts, and standardised multi-year financials in one place — the easiest place to fetch fundamentals. Primary vs. secondary data — Primary data comes straight from the company (filings, reports, transcripts). Secondary data is other people's commentary on it (news, broker notes, research reports). The pull/analysis side of this challenge must use primary data only. 3. Headline financial terms Revenue — Total sales — the top line of the P&L; the money coming in from selling goods/services. EBITDA — Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation & Amortisation. A rough proxy for operating profitability — how profitable the core business is before financing and accounting effects. PBT — Profit Before Tax — profit after all costs and interest but before income tax. PAT — Profit After Tax — the bottom line; what's left for shareholders after tax. Also called "net profit." Margin — A profit figure as a % of revenue (e.g. EBITDA margin = EBITDA ÷ revenue). Shows how much of each rupee of sales becomes profit. QoQ (quarter-on-quarter) — This quarter compared with the immediately previous quarter. YoY (year-on-year) — This quarter compared with the same quarter a year earlier — the usual way to strip out seasonality. 4. Balance-sheet, cash & ratio terms Balance sheet — A snapshot of what a company owns (assets) and owes (liabilities) at a point in time, plus shareholders' equity. Debt / leverage — Money the company has borrowed. "Leverage" describes how much debt it carries relative to its equity or earnings — more debt means more risk. Debt-to-Equity (D/E) — A leverage ratio: total debt ÷ shareholders' equity. Higher = more reliance on borrowing. Working-capital cycle — The cash tied up in day-to-day operations — inventory and money owed by customers, minus money owed to suppliers. A lengthening cycle can quietly drain cash. Cash flow — The actual cash moving in and out, as opposed to accounting profit. "Cash from operations" is the cash the core business generates.

Cash conversion — How well accounting profit turns into real cash. Persistent profit without matching cash flow is a red flag. Free cash flow (FCF) — Cash from operations minus capital spending — the cash genuinely left over. ROCE / ROE — Return on Capital Employed / Return on Equity — how much profit the company generates per rupee of capital / shareholders' money. Higher is generally better. Contingent liabilities — Potential future obligations (e.g. disputed taxes, guarantees) disclosed in the notes — not yet on the balance sheet but worth watching. Related-party transactions (RPTs) — Deals between the company and its own promoters/insiders or their other businesses — legitimate but a place to watch for value leaking out. Auditor's qualification — When the auditor flags a concern about the accounts rather than signing off cleanly — a signal to dig deeper. 5. Corporate-action & market terms NSE / BSE — India's two main stock exchanges (National / Bombay Stock Exchange). Listed companies file their announcements here. Ticker / symbol — A stock's short code. On Yahoo Finance, Indian tickers add .NS (NSE) or .BO (BSE) — e.g. RELIANCE.NS. Market capitalisation — The total market value of a company's shares (share price × number of shares). A rough measure of company size. Promoter — The controlling shareholder / founding group of an Indian listed company. Pledging — A promoter using their own shares as collateral for a loan. Heavy pledging is a risk signal. Material announcement — A disclosure significant enough to potentially move the stock, as opposed to routine compliance paperwork. Guidance — Management's own stated forward expectations — for growth, margins, capex, and so on. Capex (capital expenditure) — Spending on long-term assets like new plants or capacity. Dividend — A cash payout to shareholders from profits. Record date — The cut-off date deciding who's eligible for a dividend, bonus, or split. Bonus issue / stock split — Corporate actions that increase your share count without any cash changing hands; each share is worth proportionally less. Buyback — A company buying back its own shares from the market, usually returning surplus cash to shareholders. Rights issue / QIP / preferential allotment — Ways a company raises fresh equity — from existing shareholders (rights), from institutions (QIP = Qualified Institutional Placement), or to specific investors (preferential allotment). EOD (end-of-day) — Data taken after market close — the daily close price and traded volume used on the push side.
